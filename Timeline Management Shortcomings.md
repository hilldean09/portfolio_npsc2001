This reflection concerns *Planning and Organisation 1*.

At the beginning of this project I established an estimated timeline recorded in my Independent Study Contract (ISC), the parts of which are described briefly in *Artefact: ```mid_year_presentation_timeline.png```*. As time and the project have progressed however, I have realised how significantly flawed my timeline was. 

Before planning the timeline I had researched the various topics included in the project outline and estimated the depth of understanding and the associated time commitment I would require in attempt to remedy my previously inadequate research habits (*Problem Identification and Solution 1*). In making the timeline I was aware that to keep to it would require a higher level of consistent effort which I can comfortably say I have maintained. My initial assessment of the topics I would need to learn and the most complicated tasks in the project would turn out to be very wrong. An example of my aforementioned mispredictions, which was highlighted by Prof. Victor Calo in a meeting, is my initial assumption of needing to understanding tree-based adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) and refinements schemas in as much depth as T8code utilises, the reality would be that I would need to understand the topological data structures and algorithms in significantly more depth than I would tree-based AMR.

The cumulative inaccuracies of my early assumptions in creating the timeline has led to a large discrepancy between my progress and the timeline. As of writing I am at stage *(1.2.3)* as described in my ISC while my projected stage by the timeline is *(1.2.6)* (near completion) (*Initiative and Enterprise 2*). 

Despite my effort to better my research skills, which I identified as inadequate under *Problem Identification and Solution 1*, focusing on deeply researching a topic, I had failed to consider properly aligning my research efforts. I believe the root mistake that lead to aforementioned was not asking for more supervisor (Prof. Calo) input on my timeline and background research priorities. 

Research and proper time management is crucial to careers in high-performance computing (HPC). Research provides the foundation for any HPC and scientific endeavour, a lack of a "compass" for directing one's background research can lead to weak project foundations and much time being waster. Time management is crucial as mismanagement of compute allocations, which operate on a quarterly cycle, and sponsor/grant conditions (which may depend on certain deadlines) can quickly damage a project's longevity and the researcher's reputation.

As of recently, and extending to the future, I aim to more readily seek supervisor input on materials and foundational topics I need to understand when approaching a field I am unfamiliar with.
