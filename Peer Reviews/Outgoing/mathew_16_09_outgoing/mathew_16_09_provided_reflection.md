# The Copied Reflection

For the majority of semester one, my time put into this unit was focused on completing the numerical physics modules. These were run by Dr Jacob Martin, who is now my supervisor. The modules taught us how to model specific physics problems using different computational techniques. These were done in MATLAB, which was useful in itself as it taught me a new program I can use, expanding my programming skills.

These modules taught different techniques for many real world applications, which was very useful in understanding the reasons why we were learning certain methods. A single session would have one real world scenario that all the problems are designed to help better understand. We would learn multiple different computation methods to approach the same individual problem, and compare their results to understand which technique is most appropriate. This involved balancing both the accuracy of the results and the computation time. We also had to understand when certain methods would not work at all, and the reasons behind that.

One of the things the modules taught me was how to work in a small team. While the assessment was done individually, the whole group worked together while trying to solve a problem. When we were unsure of something, we would often discuss possible solutions, and then each try a different one. Afterwards, we would compare results and determine which of the methods was the best one. This process was good practise for me in teamwork, and was especially good since we all had similar interests, despite coming from different backgrounds.
