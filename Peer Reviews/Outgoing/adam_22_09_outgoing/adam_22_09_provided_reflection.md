# The Copied Reflection

At the start of my project my supervisor gave me some journal articles he thought would be good pre-reading before I start the project. The goal was to help give me a better understanding of what the current situation of proton therapy in Australia was like compared to the rest of the world, including technological advancements and clinical applications. I was able to read most of them and found them very interesting, however there were a few papers that were hidden behind a strict paywall. I spent a fair amount of time trying to find a workaround, I would search for the article in different search engines, but ultimately, I never found a solution. Without wanting to feel overly frustrated, I moved on assuming that I would be able to make do with the information I already had.

The next time I met my supervisor I was showing him some of my other work when the topic of one of the articles I couldn’t read came up. I explained that I couldn’t read it without paying for a seven-dollar weekly subscription. Almost instantly he introduced me to the Curtin library database. Within thirty seconds, he pulled up the article I had been looking for.

This is going to be a key turning point for my research skills. I already previously knew about the library databases; however, I never actually understood how useful they can be. As a university student, I have access to a vast repository of scientific literature that is otherwise locked to the public. Moving forward whenever I encounter a paywall or having trouble accessing specific journal articles I will now always think to immediately go straight into the Curtin library system, ensuring that my research isn’t limited by subscription barriers.
