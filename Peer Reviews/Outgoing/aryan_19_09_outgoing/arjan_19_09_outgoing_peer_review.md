# My Peer Review

I found your reflection to be a very personal insight into your approach and difficulties relating to your mid-year presentation.I found your upfront acknowledgement of your struggles and areas for future improvement to be a highlight of your reflection. Your analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of your mid-year presentation was deep and thorough, I liked how you reflected using others' mid-year presentations as reference.

I believe the area in which your reflection could be improved most is the use of specific examples to reinforce the narrative, e.g. what was a dot point or timing you restructured after the practice presentations with us physics students. Another form specific examples could take, and which would boost your reflection from both a rubric and a engagement perspective, is explicitly linked artefacts and/or references to achievement outcomes in your Independent Study Contract (ISC).

The personal writing style stood out to me and gave me a more relaxed and less filtered feeling in reading your reflection. However, I do believe it is too informal in some areas, especially in an academic assessment context, I think the most important example being "physics guys." While our physics cohort is all male and identifies as men (to the best of my knowledge) and I don't believe this would cause anyone offense, generally, gendered language is avoided in professional contexts where it has a suitable alternative, in this case "physics students" or "my physics peers" could be used. I reiterate that I do not believe this is a critical failure in this assessment, given it's personal nature, however I do believe that it is something to be aware of.

I'd definitely say your reflection is more personal and unique than any others I have read to date. Your self-criticism and the result of your introspection is clear and direct. I believe with some additional refinement (specifics examples and a editorial check) you will have little to worry about with your reflections.
