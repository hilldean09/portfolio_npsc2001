# The Copied Reflection

At the end of semester one, I presented my mid-year presentation on the project work I had been undertaking. Usually when I do public speaking, I get very nervous and often stutter or forget something important that I needed to say. For this presentation, instead of writing a script and memorising it verbatim like I usually do, I only wrote a few dot points to guide the flow of the speech. Instead of memorising a large block of text, I familiarised myself with the project work I had been doing and made sure that I knew every detail well.
 
I also usually only memorised the script in my head without properly practicing out loud. This could sometimes lead to timing issues as when spoken out loud I would often talk faster due to my nerves. However, this time I was given the opportunity to practice my presentation in front of my project supervisor and other team members of the research group. Practising out loud revealed that I was over the time limit, which allowed me to edit some unimportant parts out. Having people from planetary science, a field which I am very new to, view my presentation also assisted in correcting any inaccuracies in the content.
 
When I gave my presentation I felt much more confident that I usually do as I knew the content much better, rather than just memorising text. This also assisted in answering questions at the end, as my knowledge on my work was a at a much higher level. The content in my presentation along with slides were also much more polished due to the practice presentation. I managed to stay exactly within the time limit as well. This has taught me the importance of actually knowing the content of a presentation well, rather than just memorising a speech, as well as how important practicing out loud in front of an audience is.
