This reflection relates primarily to *Communication 2*.

My project, titled "Tree-based Adaptive Mesh Refinement in Gridap.jl" where Gridap.jl is a finite-element (FE) partial differential equation (PDE) solver, being rather technically abstract has previously been relatively difficult to explain to audience (individuals or groups) who are not familiar with numerical analysis. Over the year I have refined my method of explaining my project with a focus on engaging the audience as to not devolve into technical-jargon. My refined "script" for explaining my project can be seen in *Artefact: ```project_explaination_script.md```*. 

In my refined explanation I focus on high-level ideas, analogies, and kinesti to ground the technical concepts in more familiar. Additionally, after a conversation earlier in the year with Prof. Katarina Miljkovic regarding the issue of explaining my relatively abstract project in an accessible way, where Prof. Miljkovic noted that explanations which are too lacking in technical detail may feel unsatisfying to the audience as well, I have avoided omitting the purpose and generality of the foundational mathematical objects/techniques in my explanation (e.g. PDEs and FE). 

Being able to accessibly explain abstract mathematical/computational projects and/or research is a key skill in advancing my career in mathematics and high-performance computing (HPC) as having to explain and justify the value of your work to a non-technical audience is a frequent situation in these careers, e.g. grant applications, job interviews, getting work sponsored, etc. Intentionally not shying away from explaining my project in full, when appropriate, has helped me better understand how to engage audiences with different levels of technical understanding and how they respond to different methods of explanation. For example, I have found that the whirlpool analogy (again see *Artefact: ```project_explaination_script.md```*) and the kinesthetic H1-conformity demonstration using my fingers have been most engaging and been described as the most beneficial to multiple audiences' understandings. Additionally, I have found that emphasising the generality of applications for my project (i.e. any situation which can be modelled with a partial differential equation) is less impactful than emphasising a specific use case, e.g. simulating tailings storage facility failures.

Improving how engaging my communication is to a variety of audiences is a weak point I identified earlier in the year in my Independent Study Contract (ISC) under *Communication 2*, I can comfortably say I have demonstrated improvement in this area, evidenced by *Artefact: ```project_explaination_script.md```*, which will meaningfully benefit my future career in mathematics/HPC.
