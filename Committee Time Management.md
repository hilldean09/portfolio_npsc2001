This year I became a committee member of two student clubs, the Curtin Quant Society (CQS) and the Curtin Computer Science Student Association (ComSSA). Directing events and participating in clubs activities on top of coursework and my project has been a challenging exercise in time management from which I have developed new methods and strategies to keep myself on track (*Planning and Organisation 1 & 2*).

As busy periods for each of my commitments individually  (CQS, ComSSA, and coursework) typically coincide, avoiding and managing timing conflicts (overbookings) can become quite a task. To help with this task I have moved using a physical (paper) calendar, where discerning the relative starting and end times between events can become difficult, I have moved to using an intentionally simple and minimalistic calendar app with which I can record the start and end times of events within a given date. Additionally, I have found a tool ComSSA uses called *when2meet* to be particularly valuable, allowing multiple users to list what times they are available within a given set of dates and producing a heat map of the times and dates that are most optimal for everyone, allowing for easier and more well attended events/meetings.

Especially with my project and coursework, the number of emails I am to manage has increased notably from last year. To handle this I have begun using a system of Microsoft Outlook folders named and colours as "red", "orange", and "green". The system works by moving emails from the inbox to a respective folder based on priority, red denotes high priority, orange denotes medium priority, and green denotes emails that need no further action. The "traffic light" system, as I have heard it dubbed, has helped me avoid being overwhelmed by tasks and emails. 

Time management and deadlines have been something I've known myself to be quite bad for, as I identified in my Independent Study Contract (ISC). Additionally, I have often found finding systems and tools that I could consistently use to help my time and task management to be difficult. Adjusting to and consistently using these new time management tools -- a digital calendar, *when2meet*, and the traffic light system -- has notably reduced my stress and improved my performance as a student and a committee member. 

As with any career, including high-performance computing (HPC) and mathematics, and life in general, time management is a very valuable skills, especially when good time management helps reduce stress. I believe the aforementioned tools and systems will continue to improve and benefit me far into my career.
